4Playbooks — route each part of the toolchain to a chosen agent + model
Design position
A playbook is a reusable pipeline: each stage names who (agent) and with what (model), and what carries forward in shared memory. A trigger routes incoming direction to a playbook automatically — a question never spins up the full chain. You confirm the route on every handoff (one tap to change it), and stage cards keep the checkpoint contract: sign-offs still land in Needs you.

2Agent performance + upcoming work
What "performance" honestly means here
Not tasks/hour. The signals that matter: first-pass acceptance (checkpoints you accepted without changes), redirects per assignment (how much steering they needed), and cost per accepted checkpoint — always sliced by task type. "Improving an agent" = versioning its method (playbook, prompts, engine config) and A/B-ing the variant on similar work, so every scorecard shows which method version earned the numbers.

1Fort — landing surface, three directions
Design position
The primary question isn't "what is running?" — it's "what needs me?" Running-and-fine earns a quiet pulse; blocked-on-you earns the biggest pixels. Progress = checkpoints you accepted, never an agent's own %. Base type moves from 11–13px mono to 14–15px sans; mono is reserved for data (machines, ids, times).
